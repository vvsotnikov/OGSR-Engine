# Dispersed stalker population stress test

The five fresh-save runs completed without crashes or fatal/script-error markers. This is a stationary, dispersed-population test on the Bar, not a dense visible crowd or a controlled battle. It does not establish a maximum supported population.

## Method

Each run loads a private copy of `bar_center.sav`, enables whole-map mode, waits 15 seconds after readiness, creates the requested batch, waits another 15 seconds, measures 30 seconds, then quits normally. The actor and camera are left untouched. Spawn positions use a local fixed PRNG seed of 12345, accessible current-map navigation nodes with valid cross-table game vertices, at least 40 metres from the actor and 3 metres apart. Larger batches use the same initial placement sequence. The normal `spawn_item("stalker", ...)` path is used; script behavior, combat and mortality remain enabled. Valid navigation nodes are not a guarantee of safe or story-appropriate placement. Populations can change during the run.

The same separate diagnostic executable was used for every point: SHA-256 `1AE3EEFD33EB3D80505D4D7228F47C3D3907685DAFED1DBCF4FBA9318A23A6C0`, based on `b4250067f` with the archived stress probe. Build configuration is ReleaseTracyProfiler, but **no Tracy collector was connected during the five comparison runs**. Frames come from the engine's high-resolution unscaled frame timer, written to a buffered CSV. This avoids the very large connected-Tracy event load but is still an instrumented build with some measurement overhead. Do not combine its absolute timings with earlier connected-Tracy playtests as if they were the same measurement setup.

Machine: Ryzen 7 5800X (8 cores/16 threads), RTX 4090, 3840x2160 output/render domain. Common Bar seed SHA-256: `82B9C9708B0015C4C635B1C3D0E92B120F71AF25402E8993327575A0832D3DED`. Original seed, bin_experiment and bin_validation hashes were rechecked and remain unchanged. No compilation or trace-analysis process ran during the measured windows.

All requested IDs were retained and online throughout each measured window; online here includes dead bodies. No measured frame exceeded 33.33 ms. Mean frame time grew from 4.31 ms at baseline to 10.68 ms at +400 (roughly 232 to 94 frames/second). The +50 median being slightly lower is not evidence of a speedup: these are single stochastic runs, not repeated trials with confidence intervals.

The synchronous spawn-call batches took 0, 40.68, 68.11, 136.41 and 264.05 ms respectively. The subsequent warmup includes their next-frame effect and online activation. These are deliberate whole-batch creation stalls, not proof that ordinary level loading or distance switching causes exactly the same stalls. They do motivate amortizing bulk population creation.

Average ALife switch work per update grew from 0.443 ms to 1.371 ms; offline-scheduled ALife work remained about 0.037–0.051 ms per update. These metrics do **not** include all online stalker thinking or physics and cannot by themselves identify the dominant bottleneck.

Each contains its manifest, original log, individual spawn coordinates/IDs, population samples, `stress-frames.csv` and `stress-summary.json`. The separate `bin_stress` package retains matching PDB/binaries and build log. For a rebuild, apply `stress-diagnostic.patch` and copy `StressValidation.inl` to `ogsr_engine/xrGame/`, then package separately. The patch and source are not applied to normal gameplay sources. Current repository bin_x64 output is the diagnostic build, not an unmodified gameplay build.

`Run-PopulationStress.ps1 -Counts 0,50,100,200,400` runs the isolated sweep. `Summarize-PopulationStress.ps1 -Session <path>` derives nearest-rank frame percentiles and separates the measured window from warmup. `-CaptureTrace` runs an additional connected-Tracy session beginning at the measurement marker; treat it separately from the comparison sweep.

Next workloads should distinguish a dense visible crowd, combat, and dispersed simulation. Scheduler lateness/update cadence also needs measurement: `CSheduler::ProcessStep` stops when its time budget is exceeded, so a stable frame rate does not establish unchanged AI responsiveness. This test also does not replace the pending natural level transition/task hand-in pass or validate AMK simulation.

## Additional detailed trace

Session `2026-10-03_13-26-40-160-whole-map` repeats +400 with Tracy connecting only after `measure_begin`. The recorder saved 270,193,728 bytes and the game exited normally. All 400 spawned IDs remained online; 397–400 were alive during the measurement. Recording increased native mean frame time to 12.45 ms versus 10.68 ms in the untraced +400 run, so this repeat is excluded from the sweep table. Random simulation variation is also present; the difference is not an isolated overhead estimate.

The selected trace interval is 48–70 seconds after trace origin, inside the steady-state period and clear of initial connection and shutdown. It contains 1,765 complete frames, median 12.14 ms, p99 22.73 ms and maximum 24.54 ms.

These are inclusive elapsed scopes, some nested and some running on parallel workers. **Do not sum these rows or interpret them as exclusive CPU percentages.** They identify candidate investigation areas, not a proven single bottleneck. Per-frame stalker work is substantial compared with the explicitly named scheduled brain scope. The trace does not measure per-NPC scheduling lateness, nor establish that all NPCs receive equivalent update frequency. About 935 stalker scheduled-update calls per second occurred across the population; aggregate call counts alone cannot diagnose starvation.

`TraceSummary.cpp` now also exports `stalker/` scopes. The resulting zone projection is about 393 MB. The original PowerShell materializing/grouping analysis was stopped before completion because of its resource cost; raw evidence was preserved. `Summarize-TraceStream.py` processes it incrementally using only Python's standard library, storing numeric durations rather than millions of row objects. It completed in about seven seconds. Frame and zone statistics were independently compared with the previously generated paired-Bar summary and matched within 0.000001 ms. Use `python Summarize-TraceStream.py <prefix> 48 70` to reproduce this window; the JSON result is `stress-trace-stream-summary.json` in the session.
